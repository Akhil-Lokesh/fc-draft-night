# FC Draft Night — The Story

2026-09-20 · @Someone

Five friends. Five real clubs. One hour to wreck and rebuild every one of them before a ball gets kicked.

## The pitch

Every FC 26 or FC 27 custom tournament starts the same dull way: everyone picks a club, everyone plays with the roster the game hands them, and the night's most interesting decision — who gets Mbappé — never actually happens, because whoever clicks Real Madrid first just gets him for free. Draft night fixes that. Before the tournament starts, the five of you turn squad-building into the actual competition: same shared rules, same ticking clock, real players changing hands in real bidding wars, and nobody walks up to kickoff with the team they started the evening with.

## Setting up

You're hosting, so you open the app, name the room, and set the total budget. The app already knows what each real club's squad is actually worth, and it won't let you lowball the number — Real Madrid's real squad runs 545M, so the app won't let you set the tournament budget below 600M, or nobody could even afford to keep the team they started with. You land on 600M and send the room code out. Your four friends join, each claiming a club — Arsenal, Bayern Munich, Barcelona, Manchester City — and the app locks in their real starting squads along with the budget each one reserves automatically. Real Madrid's squad alone reserves 425M of their 600M, leaving them just 175M spendable; a club with a cheaper real squad walks in with far more room to spend from the first second.

## The room goes live

The hour starts, and within the first minute three separate things are already happening at once. Arsenal lists a direct offer on Wirtz, sitting unclaimed in the pool, at his 60M listed price. Real Madrid quietly lists Kroos back to the pool at his own listed value, deciding he's not worth the roster spot this season. And out of nowhere, all five managers start piling onto Haaland, whose 180M price tag makes him the most valuable name left in the pool. Wirtz's two-minute window closes with nobody challenging it — Arsenal signs him outright for 60M. Kroos's window closes the same way — he's a free agent now, sitting in the pool at his old price, up for grabs by anyone.

## The fight for Haaland

Haaland turns into the fight of the night. City opens at 181M, Barcelona jumps to 190M, Arsenal answers at 195M, Bayern goes to 200M, and City comes back at 205M — their second quote, so they're capped out right there whether they win or not. Arsenal raises once more to 215M, spending their own last quote. Bayern closes it out at 220M. Nobody else can raise — City and Arsenal have both burned through their two allowed quotes, Barcelona never came back for a second bid, Real Madrid never got involved at all. Five minutes of silence pass, and Haaland belongs to Bayern for the rest of the season.

## A costly mistake

Midway through the hour, Manchester City gets greedy. They're the top bidder on both Pedri (90M) and Musiala (80M) at the same time — 170M combined, more than their spendable can actually cover. The Pedri deal closes first and goes through cleanly. When the Musiala deal tries to close right after, City can't cover it — the deal voids on the spot, Musiala reverts to Bayern untouched, and City eats a flat 25M fine on top of losing the player outright. It's the overcommitment rule doing exactly what it exists to do: reaching for more than you can actually afford has a real, visible cost, not just an inconvenience.

## Playing it smart

Barcelona, still stinging from losing Haaland, decides to lean on Real Madrid instead — challenging Mbappé, then Vinícius, then Bellingham, one right after another. Real Madrid successfully defends all three, and each successful defense permanently resets that player's price higher heading into next season. By the third challenge, Barcelona has used all three of the challenges they're allowed against Real Madrid specifically this season — the app won't let them open a fourth fight against anyone else on that squad, even with plenty of spendable still sitting in their budget. They pivot back to the pool instead, and Real Madrid's remaining players get to breathe.

## Closing the room

The hour runs out with a few fights still live — whoever's sitting on top when the clock hits zero wins at that price, no exceptions, no grace period. Final squads lock in, and every manager gets a clean summary: who they picked up, who they lost, and what's left to carry into next season. Now the only manual part left: everyone opens FC 27's Team Management on their own console and sets their real club's roster to match their drafted squad — swapping in the players they won, moving out the ones they lost. Ten minutes of admin, and then the custom tournament kicks off for real — five real clubs, five squads nobody could have picked off a shelf, built entirely by the five of you an hour earlier.

## Between seasons

The tournament plays out, and somebody wins it. At season's end, the app exports everything that happened — every listing, every fight, final squads and spendable — with blank fields for where each manager actually finished. You fill those in and re-upload the file before next season's draft. Finishing position sets everyone's new base budget in 20M steps — last place stays at 600M, the champion starts at 680M — and whatever spendable a manager didn't spend last season carries forward on top of that. Real Madrid's Mbappé, now permanently priced at whatever it cost them to survive Barcelona's three challenges, is still theirs unless they choose to let him go. The room reopens, the clock starts again, and it's draft night once more — except this time, everyone's walking in with a season of history already on the board.
